## 2. Key Commercial Insights

### 1. The Underpricing Phase (Nov 2025 – March 2026)
A review of transaction line items reveals that the customer was invoiced for gas below cost across all cylinder sizes.
* **9kg cylinders** were invoiced at R174.44 (March 2026) against an average cost of R177.43, resulting in a **-1.7% gross margin** (a loss of R2.99 per cylinder).
* **19kg cylinders** were invoiced at R368.26 against an average cost of R376.70, resulting in a **-2.4% gross margin** (a loss of R8.44 per cylinder).
* **14kg cylinders** were invoiced at R271.35 against an average cost of R278.04, resulting in a **-2.5% gross margin** (a loss of R6.69 per cylinder).
* **48kg cylinders** were invoiced at R922.00 against an average cost of R945.07, resulting in a **-2.5% gross margin** (a loss of R23.07 per cylinder).

This represented a systemic pricing error that was losing money on every kilogram sold.

### 2. The Price Correction and Volume Shock (May 2026)
In May 2026, the company implemented a price correction to recover margins:
* **9kg cylinders** were raised from R174.44 to R232.45 (+33.3% increase).
* **19kg cylinders** were raised from R368.26 to R490.73 (+33.3% increase).

While this restored the gross margin to **19.9%** (9kg) and **20.3%** (19kg), the customer responded by immediately withholding orders. Volume fell from **1,275.00 kg** in March to **0 kg** in April, and only **275.00 kg** in May (representing a single order of 20 x 9kg and 5 x 19kg cylinders).

## 3. Risks

### 1. High Churn Risk (Buying Strike)
The customer's 22-day gap since their last order (12 May 2026) is **10 days overdue** compared to their historical average order interval of 13.2 days. Combined with a 0 kg volume in April, this indicates that the customer is actively refusing to buy at the new rates and is likely sourcing from competitors.

### 2. Commercial Deadlock
Reverting to the previous pricing structure would mean selling at a loss, which is operationally unsustainable. However, maintaining the current +33.3% price hike will result in complete account dormancy.

### 3. Credit / Balance Risk (Low)
There is no credit risk from the customer. The consolidated account currently holds a credit balance of **R137,694.86** due to overpayments and deposit credits. This credit balance represents a potential liability if the customer decides to close the account and request a refund.

---

## 4. Opportunities

### 1. Retention-Focused Wholesale Restructuring
Since the customer is highly price-sensitive and operates at a wholesale level, there is an opportunity to transition them to a structured wholesale agreement with a **10% gross margin target**. This allows us to cover costs while offering a meaningful price reduction to win back their volume.

### 2. 14kg and 48kg Alignment
There were no transactions for 14kg and 48kg sizes in May 2026. When the customer resumes buying, their prices must be adjusted to cover costs (10% target margin) rather than leaving them at the historical loss-making rates.
